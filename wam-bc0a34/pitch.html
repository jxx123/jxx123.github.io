06 · An idea old enough to be true — twice

知之为知之，不知为不知，是知也。

“To know what you know, and to know what you do not know — that is knowledge.” — Confucius, c. 500 BC

“How will you search for a thing when you do not know what it is?” — Plato, the Meno, 385 BC. Both traditions, a century apart, reached the same principle: intelligence begins with an accurate map of your own ignorance.

Two philosophies figured this out 2,400 years ago. We built it into a robot.

A1 · Model architecture

A two-level world-action model

Backbone (rented, then owned at the margin): an open VLM (~4B) continued-pretrained on an interleaved corpus of video tokens, latent-action tokens, state bins, and language under Policy / World / Dynamics templates, plus an outcome-conditioned “try-your-best” template (advantage conditioning at CPT level; A3). Dense features for the action system come from a separate frozen encoder (A9).

Latent-action model (LAM, owned): IDM/FDM over video. IDM: frame pairs → discrete latent action z (FSQ codebook). FDM: (frame, z) → next latent frame. Hard-won design rules:

  • State-free IDM/FDM. Proprioception never enters the LAM as input — human video has none, and masked-input variants partition z-space by data source (our F1/F3 failure; theory concurs: the bottleneck spends capacity on whatever best explains next-frame prediction). State appears only as masked auxiliary targets.
  • Semantic-feature inputs. IDM reads frozen dense features (DINOv3-class), not reconstruction latents — the convergent choice of every working cross-domain LAM; also our rotation-blindness (F15) fix candidate.
  • Cross-embodiment gate. One LAM controls both Franka-LIBERO and SO-101; z mixing verified visually (z-transplant grids, PCA) and numerically (leakage bits, shared mass, probe accuracy). Numbers gate, pictures verify.

Decoder (per-embodiment, quality-agnostic): flow/diffusion head (z, state, visual latent) → action chunks. An embodiment adapter, not a policy: trains on any interaction data including autonomous play — which keeps new-embodiment adaptation cheap.

Judgment heads: verdict (calibrated success probability, three-way; A4) and progress (a value function — dense, not binary). Trained on fleet-verified outcomes.

Solid accent = trained by us. Dashed = frozen or optional. Amber = the frozen vocabulary contract (the cliff). Below the red line: supervised or small-Gaussian RL only. Fleet logs feed back into the source tier — the loop closes through the real world.

A3 · RL doctrine

Exact likelihoods wherever RL happens

Every learning update is one of three shapes: (a) GRPO over a categorical (z / verdict tokens on the backbone); (b) PPO over a small Gaussian (magnitude-clamped residual on the frozen decoder); (c) weighted regression (supervised flow-matching decoder refresh). Policy gradients through the flow head (FPO/DiPOD lineage) are excluded by default: documented surrogate drift, 2–5× per-gradient cost, and no remaining job in a two-level architecture with a discrete decision interface.

The gate is measured, not argued: entropy_below_z — the share of state-residualized action variance the codes do NOT capture — is a headline verify metric. Low → tier (a) sees every decision that matters. Rising on contact dims → residual tier, periodically distilled back into the decoder. Multimodal below-z residuals → neither RL tier: mode selection is leaking below the vocabulary; fix = finer codebook at the next scheduled bump.

Advantage conditioning lives at CPT level (outcome-conditioned template — uses failures instead of discarding them; shares labels with verdict training). The flow level stays advantage-agnostic: weighting there only acts on below-z variation (same gate) and would break the decoder’s task-free play-data diet.

Fleet training is iterated offline RL kept near-on-policy by cadence (30-min LoRA loop): filtered/advantage-conditioned BC + DAgger-by-escalation (takeovers = expert actions on the deployed policy’s own state distribution). True on-policy rollouts only in sim and imagination, verifier-guarded. Verdict calibration is policy-conditional: every redeploy starts conservative and re-temperatures on first fresh episodes.

A4 · The judgment layer

The verifier program — success, progress, blame, next question

  • Verdict head, three-way: autonomous / assistable / beyond-current-capability. The third class (ZPD boundary) stops the fleet grinding out-of-zone tasks. Measured dynamically: dose-response probes (does one in-context demo move the score?), z-vocabulary coverage of a teacher demo, sequential learning-progress tests under a help-seconds budget, failure localization. A fourth escalation type — instruction ambiguity (ask a clarifying question, not a demo) — adopted from Introspective Planning (2024).
  • Progress head = value function: dense score, not binary — needed for learning-progress (a derivative), mid-episode escalation on stall rather than after failure, imagination ranking, RLVR shaping. Pretrainable from actionless video (VIP/GVL-style); anchored by binary outcomes; anti-hacking via temporal-contrastive training. Two monitors, not one (Sentinel lesson): erratic-behavior (distributional) and task-progress (semantic) failures are caught by different detectors.
  • Label-the-labeler: periodic human audit of auto-verdicts; audit disagreement is a standing gate.
  • Ordering rule: borrow judgment before action — human verdict labels (seconds) are cheaper than demonstrations (minutes).

Structural claim behind the moat: success-biased corpora cannot train a calibrated judge — failure examples are prerequisites, and dojo self-play manufactures them deliberately.

A5 · World model

Fast imagination, honestly guarded

The FDM doubles as an offline simulator. Direction: one-step generation (DriftWorld-class drifting loss) as a drop-in upgrade over the MSE one-step baseline at unchanged inference cost — reprices imagination-RLVR (~17× cheaper rollouts) and enables deployment-time lookahead on-device.

  • Offline policy ranking: candidate LoRA updates rolled out in imagination before fleet push — a pre-deploy gate for the 30-min loop (published analogs: 0.92–0.99 rank correlation; requires failure-case training, which self-play supplies).
  • Replay-consistency test: feed logged real actions; divergence from logged frames separates world-model error from policy error — each routes to a different data purchase.
  • Guards: short horizons, verifier/model ensembles, logged-episode replay as the reality check.

A6 · Data proposer (Phase 2 collection)

The model writes next week’s shopping list

score(candidate) = P(failure) × E[learning progress] × transfer breadth ÷ cost

Never failure alone (the noisy-TV trap: always-failing, never-improving regions are out-of-ZPD). Generators, each blind differently: verdict-scored imagination sweeps; perturbation boundary mapping; fleet escalation clusters; vocabulary diagnostics. A bandit allocates the weekly budget; output is a structured proposal a human vetoes in thirty seconds. Double-model error handled by replay-consistency + a five-rollout dojo spot-check; proposer precision is a standing audit. The proposer trains on its own realized outcomes — collection taste compounds. Staging: v0 rule-based; v1 adds imagination; v2 adds boundary mapping + the learned proposer.

A7 · Verification discipline

Numbers gate, pictures verify

Every model version runs a standard battery before its gates are trusted; artifacts are regenerable, resumable, and double as flywheel data. Institutionalized lesson from v1: per-embodiment quantitative gates looked fine while z-space was fully embodiment-partitioned — only cross-embodiment visualization exposed it.

  • Pictures: z-transplant clip grids, z-PCA by embodiment/task, code-usage heatmaps, FDM reconstruction videos, action-prediction overlays.
  • Numbers: action-information capture in bits (state-conditioned ANOVA), embodiment-leakage bits (want ~0), entropy_below_z, decoder z-reliance (counterfactual code swap), error-placement metrics (flip-chunk MAE), calibration audits.
  • Discipline: checkpoints are bundles (weights + config + architecture contract + lineage); pipelines constructed FROM the bundle; training-owned keys cannot be overridden at eval; embodiment-spec version mismatch refuses to load.

A9 · Encoders & pretrain corpus

Perception choices, empirically gated

Dense features: DINOv3 distilled tier (S+/B) — the field’s convergent choice for dense prediction and robot feature spaces (license review before product dependence). Video-native ablation: V-JEPA 2.1. Watch: EUPE (≤89M universal encoders) for on-device economics — research-licensed, benched pending terms. CLIP-family encoders excluded from the IDM path (weak dense features, appearance bias). The decision is an afternoon: the probe battery runs over all candidates on the 1–2k-hr subset.

Corpus (~10k hrs, composition-first): natural human video + open robot corpora (free bulk); owned instrument data (accurate EE + force at v2); recovery-dense protocol — perturbations scripted, fumbles paid for, never filtered; dojo self-play for the failure tail. Ingestion prerequisites scoped: camera-motion filtering, stabilization, frame-gap normalization. All raw streams are model-independent by construction: the LAM relabels every stream into the current vocabulary, retroactively — collection sits above the cliff.

A10 · Strategy reviews, contingencies, kill criteria

What would change our mind, written down in advance

  • Scale-first vs depth-first: pure scale-first is dominated for us (capital game; open-loop corpora structurally exclude the closed-loop tail; defers rather than avoids vertical work). Pure depth-first is fragile without a transfer carrier. Adopted: rent breadth, own depth — antifragile to the other path succeeding. Expansion tracked as demos-to-competence per vertical; adjacency measured in z-space.
  • Recovery-emergence watch: (a) recovery behaviors will emerge from scale (~80% by 2028–30; good for us — escalation baseline drops); (b) contact-precision recovery is sensing-gated (instrument v2 force); (c) policy-specific failure awareness never emerges from open-loop data (self-referential); (d) site drift is non-stationary. When (a) moves in published benchmarks, re-price the wedge’s escalation baseline.
  • Closed-frontier contingency: closed APIs cannot enter air-gapped, certification-bound sites; fallback breadth = open fast-follower tier + own corpus; our bundle/verify infra makes us the fastest integrator of any open release.

A11 · Why LLMs don’t know what they don’t know

The IDK literature, and why our setting escapes its trap

The abstention/calibration literature converges on a diagnosis: (1) pretraining has no IDK gradient — hallucination rate lower-bounded by singleton facts (Kalai et al. 2025, Nature 2026); (2) post-training anti-calibrates — binary-graded evals and raters reward guessing; (3) parametric knowledge has no index — self-knowledge is a weak probeable correlate (AUROC ~0.75–0.85); (4) internal calibration isn’t routed to verbalization; (5) the boundary moves with every fine-tune, and training beyond the model’s knowledge actively teaches hallucination (Gekhman 2024); (6) no grounded verification loop — text has no cheap verifier outside math/code.

Point 6 inverts for a deployed fleet: every episode ends in physical ground truth verified by a paying customer; a narrow ODD makes in-distribution calibration feasible; self-play manufactures the failure examples that must never be faked; conformal wrappers (KnowNo lineage) convert verdict scores into statistical guarantees; and the field’s proposed fix — grade confident errors negatively, reward calibrated abstention — is our fleet’s native grading, with the abstention operating point set by escalation economics. Design rules adopted: separate verdict head, never verbalized-only; per-version IDK data from consistency probing; never fine-tune competence the decoder can’t ground; ensemble/consistency features as verdict inputs; conformal layer on top; out-of-scope column in every eval.
