The bets, in one paragraph each

π0.5 bets that one big co-trained model plus enormous action-labeled teleop is the road: keep semantics in the backbone (web co-training, knowledge insulation so the flow expert can't overwrite them), generate actions end-to-end, and buy generalization with data scale. It is the best-evidenced bet in the field — and its costs are structural: every hour of capability needs action-labeled robot data, every new embodiment touches the whole model, and nothing in the architecture knows when it's wrong.

Apprentice bets that the action interface should be a small discrete vocabulary learned from unlabeled video, so the expensive tiers of the data pyramid train most of the model for free; that a per-embodiment decoder makes embodiment adaptation cheap and contract-safe; that verdict/progress heads and a world model belong in the architecture from day one because deployability — not benchmark success — is the product; and that discrete tokens make RL exact instead of approximate. The honest status: the bet's machinery is built and measured, and its first full test (the 12-code vocabulary) already taught us the vocabulary must be rotation-aware and the backbone trained to convergence — which is exactly what this week's lever A and R2 corpus are for.
